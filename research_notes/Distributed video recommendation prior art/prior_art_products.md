# Prior art: decentralized and user-controlled recommendations (video focus) and the "follow + match / shared history" idea

Scope note: research done 2026-10-19 with about 17 search/fetch calls, then a verification pass the same day (see the revision log at the end). Items marked **[verified]** were checked against a fetched page, source code, or search results. Items marked **[background, not re-verified]** come from general knowledge. Their URLs are canonical project pages, but the specific claims were not re-checked, so the report writer should hedge them. Items marked **[unresolved]** were checked but could not be settled; the reason is given.

## Has anyone built this exact thing? Follow creators, plus a "match" relationship where chosen peers exchange watch/like history to drive video recommendations, on an open or decentralized substrate

### Takeaway
I found no shipped product or protocol that combines all three parts: (a) following creators, (b) an explicit, consented peer "match" link through which two or more users exchange private watch/like history, and (c) recommendations computed from that shared history on a decentralized or user-controlled substrate. Each part exists on its own. Centralized, closed services pair (b) and (c): Spotify Blend, YouTube Music Taste Match, Plex Discover Together, and historically Last.fm neighbours. The only working decentralized version of (b)+(c) is Tribler's BuddyCast "taste buddies" from 2006–2008, which was P2P collaborative filtering over download histories, used gossip with similar peers, and was not opt-in per peer. By 2012 Tribler described Dispersy as BuddyCast's successor. On the open protocols (Nostr, ATproto, ActivityPub), recommendation work centers on public social-graph trust (follows) and public engagement. None of them has a standard way to share private watch history with chosen peers. ATproto's permissioned-data work ("Atproto Spaces", proposal 0016) opened an alpha in Aug 2026, but it provides access control, not end-to-end encryption. The nearest server-side analogues of "taste matching" are a few co-like feeds: Graze's open-source personalization engine (random walks over the Bluesky like graph) and Funkwhale's pod-local "similar" radio, which mines other users' listening history on the same server, limited to users whose privacy setting allows it.

### Cited Findings

**Closest analogues: consented taste-sharing between chosen people (centralized)**
- **Spotify Blend** [verified]. One user invites another, and Spotify generates a shared playlist from both users' tastes that refreshes daily. A "taste match score" is shown as a percentage, along with the song that brings the two together. - **YouTube Music "Taste Match"** [verified, thin]. You send an invite link to a friend and get a combined playlist. This is the closest video-company analogue, but it covers music only. — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/how-to-taste-match-your-youtube-music-playlist-with-a-friend)
- **Plex Discover Together** [verified]. It went public in Nov 2023 as a social layer: watch history, watchlist and ratings can each be set to Private or Friends Only. Friends' activity shows in an Activity feed, and title pages show which friends watched or rated a title. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/01/plex-becomes-a-social-network-with-public-debut-of-discover-together); [Plex forum](https://forums.plex.tv/t/discover-together-public-release/857227)

This caused a backlash, and Plex added an opt-out. — [Cord Cutters News](https://cordcuttersnews.com/this-new-plex-feature-shares-your-weird-watch-history-with-friends-and-its-tricky-to-turn-off/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2023/11/28/plex_privacy/)
  - It covers only activity on Plex. It does not import history from other streaming services. — [Cord Cutters News](https://cordcuttersnews.com/plexs-new-discover-together-makes-the-app-more-like-social-media/)
- **Last.fm neighbours** [verified for existence]. **Closest decentralized analogue: Tribler BuddyCast (P2P "taste buddies")**
- Tribler (TU Delft) ran a decentralized recommender that compared download histories to find "taste buddies" and recommended files they had that you lacked. Tribler describes it as decentralized user-based collaborative filtering. — [Tribler: DecentralizedRecommendation](https://tribler.org/DecentralizedRecommendation)
- How it worked: each peer keeps a list of its most similar peers and a list of random peers. It periodically gossips with a taste buddy (exploitation) or a random peer (exploration). BuddyCast3 added ratings and comments. — [Tribler: Buddycast3](https://tribler.org/Buddycast3); [Tribler paper, TU/e](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/tribler-a-social-based-peer-to-peer-system/)
- The approach was applied to P2P TV, predicting interest from zapping (channel-switching) behaviour. — [CWI](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/13818)
- Status [verified in part]:
  - A 2012 ACM SIGMM open-source column on Tribler says: "Dispersy is the successor of BuddyCast, and it is focused on NAT-traversal." In 2012 BuddyCast still connected "to a new peer every 15s" to exchange preference lists. — [SIGMM Records, 19 Jul 2012](https://sigmm.hosting.acm.org/2012/07/19/open-source-column-tribler-p2p-search-share-and-stream/)
  - The current Tribler README describes "P2P content discovery" and does not mention BuddyCast. — [Tribler/tribler](https://github.com/Tribler/tribler)
  - The earlier citation, a Tribler forum reply saying "Taste-Buddies algorithms" were removed about a decade ago, could not be re-checked. forum.tribler.org now redirects (HTTP 301) to GitHub Discussions, and the Wayback Machine was rate-limited from this session. I kept the old URL only as a historical pointer: forum.tribler.org/t/clarification-needed-regarding-cooperative-downloading/7064. Context: [Pouwelse et al., "fifteen year struggle"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.4818)
- Lessons: the design is technically close (history-sharing with similar peers, gossip). But history was shared with algorithmically chosen peers rather than consented "matches", which raises privacy issues, and the feature did not survive.

**Academic P2P collaborative filtering (no shipping products found)**
- P2PCF (2021): each peer sees only its friends' interactions, and recommendations are computed locally. The design aims at privacy and cold start. This is the closest academic match to "share with chosen peers". — [SAGE / JHS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.3233/JHS-210649)
- TU Darmstadt (2009): a P2P recommender that restricts information exchange to friends and avoids super-peers. — [PDF](https://www.kom.tu-darmstadt.de/papers/PKFS09_567.pdf)
- PipeCF and DHT-based distributed CF: distributed CF scales comparably to centralized CF. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417404000065); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221000888_Distributed_Collaborative_Filtering_for_Peer-to-Peer_File_Sharing_Systems)
- An extended search for an open-protocol app that shares watch history with friends found no such open-source product. — search conducted 2026-10-19 (no URL; negative result)

### Inferences
- The "match" concept is validated as a consumer feature in centralized form (Spotify Blend, Taste Match, Plex) and was technically demonstrated P2P (Tribler). The new part would be consented, pairwise, private history exchange plus local CF on an open protocol, applied to video.
- What went wrong in the analogues: Plex's all-or-nothing visibility and default-on emails, and Tribler's non-consensual similarity gossip. Both suggest per-item or per-peer controls and explicit opt-in.
- Blend's visible match percentage and "the song that brings you together" are the parts users share socially. That makes them a likely good UX pattern to borrow.

### Gaps
- Last.fm neighbours: the page route survives but appears empty, and the API method is gone. Whether the feature is formally retired is [unresolved] (see above).
- Spotify Blend's algorithm is undisclosed.
- I did not check whether any Nostr or ATproto hobby project implements "shared watch history with chosen follows". GitHub code search was out of scope for this pass.

## Fediverse (ActivityPub): PeerTube, Sepia Search, PeerTube Picks, Loops, Owncast, Funkwhale, Mastodon discovery

### Takeaway
PeerTube itself has no personalized recommendation engine. Its built-in "recommended/next videos" list comes from the current PeerTube client code. It shows up to 5 videos from the instance's own search (`searchTarget: 'local'`) that share at least one tag with the current video, newest first. Logged-in users' already-watched videos are excluded. If that search finds one video or fewer, it falls back to the instance's newest videos. It is tag- and recency-based, not random, although an undated fairkom issue described it as random (see below). Personalization exists only in third-party, client-side experiments that keep history local and share nothing (PeerTube Picks). Loops (fediverse TikTok-style) has a For You feed using engagement, hashtags and social-graph signals. Fediverse discovery is mostly server-side trending plus search indexes. The one federated-media exception is Funkwhale's "similar" radio, which does collaborative filtering over the pod's own listening history.

### Cited Findings
- **PeerTube Picks** [verified]. A Firefox add-on that recommends PeerTube videos using cosine similarity over the videos you watched and liked. Watch history stays in the browser, with export/import/delete in its Options page. It contacts servers only to fetch video metadata. It was announced by Cattail@lemmy.world. — [PieFed post](https://piefed.jeena.net/post/169216)
  - Source and license (resolved): the Mozilla Add-ons API lists the add-on under author "solidheron", version 1.0, MIT License, homepage `github.com/solidheron/peertube_recomendation_algorythm`. It was created 2025-05-08, last updated 2025-05-11, and had about 4 average daily users on 2026-10-19. It is therefore the Firefox build of the Chromium extension below. — [AMO listing](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/peertube-picks/); [AMO API](https://addons.mozilla.org/api/v5/addons/addon/peertube-picks/)
- **PeerTube recommendation algorithm (alpha)** [verified]. A Chromium extension by the same author, with repo `solidheron/peertube_recomendation_algorythm`. It pulls videos from several instances and ranks by "like similarity" and "time engagement similarity" (cosine similarity over English title/description/tag words). It halves the scores of already-seen videos and caps results at 5 per channel. Data is stored in IndexedDB, for a single user only, with "no data sharing yet". — [PieFed post](https://piefed.jeena.net/post/158506); [GitHub](https://github.com/solidheron/peertube_recomendation_algorythm)
  - Relevance: this is the closest open-source video project to the idea. It already has the local profile; what it lacks is peer exchange.
- **PeerTube-Browser** [verified, thin]. A third-party site whose recommendations use embedding vectors, approximate-nearest-neighbour (ANN) search, and user likes. — [PieFed](https://piefed.jeena.net/post/326824)
- **Recommender issue (displ.eu/fairkom)** [verified, thin]. Proposes a PeerTube plugin to build a sorted recommendation list from local and federated content. It wants episodes ordered alphanumerically rather than "randomly (as currently implemented in PeerTube)". — [fairkom GitLab](https://git.fairkom.net/emb/displ.eu/recommender/-/issues/7)
  - Reconciled with the code: PeerTube's recommender is not literally random. `VideoRecommendationService` asks for 5 videos. It runs an empty-query local search filtered by `tagsOneOf` (the current video's tags), sorted `-publishedAt` (newest first), with `excludeAlreadyWatched` for logged-in users. It falls back to the newest-videos list when the search returns ≤1 result, or when the instance turns off local search in favour of a remote search index. From a viewer's side, "newest video that shares any tag" can look arbitrary, which probably explains the fairkom wording. The point that matters is unchanged: there is no series/episode ordering and no personalization. — [video-recommendation.service.ts (develop)](https://github.com/Chocobozzz/PeerTube/blob/develop/client/src/app/%2Bvideo-watch/shared/recommendations/video-recommendation.service.ts)
- **Loops** [verified]. Open-source short-video app by Daniel Supernault (Pixelfed). It added ActivityPub federation on 14 Oct 2025. It has a chronological Following feed and a For You feed that, per the project, draws on engagement, hashtags and social-graph signals (self-description). Wikipedia notes human-led "trust scores" for moderation. The iOS app launch date was reported as 30 Jan 2026. — [joinloops.org features](https://joinloops.org/features); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loops_(app)); [roadmap](https://joinloops.org/roadmap)
- **Sepia Search** [verified]. sepiasearch.org describes itself as "A search engine of PeerTube videos, channels and playlists, developed by Framasoft". Its code (the `search-index` repo, "Application behind search.joinpeertube.org") is TypeScript/Node with Express, a Vue 3 client and Meilisearch. It is global keyword search, not personalized recommendations. — [sepiasearch.org](https://sepiasearch.org); [framagit search-index README](https://framagit.org/framasoft/peertube/search-index)
- **Owncast** [verified]. Self-hosted livestreaming with fediverse integration. Discovery is an optional, opt-in directory: a server "registers by pinging the directory every 4 minutes, and only while you are live". The docs note anyone can build their own directory from the live status Owncast servers broadcast over ActivityPub. I found no personalized recommendation feature. — [Owncast docs: The Directory](https://owncast.online/docs/directory/)
- **Funkwhale** [verified; corrects the earlier "no recommendation features"]. Federated audio. Its "radios" include a registered `similar` radio, a pod-local collaborative filter. For a seed track (the session's last 3 tracks, then the start track), it queries `history_listening` with SQL to find which track listeners most often played next. It counts only listeners whose `privacy_level` is `instance` or `everyone`, plus the current user, and picks randomly among the matches. Funkwhale also has a `troi` radio that calls ListenBrainz's Troi recommender using the user's ListenBrainz credentials. The user docs list only Favorites, Random, Recently Added, Less listened and "Your content" as built-in radios, so `similar` is mainly reached from track/artist pages. This is the closest existing federated-media precedent for "recommend from other people's listening history, gated by their privacy setting", but it is server-side and instance-wide, not consented per peer. — [radios.py (develop)](https://dev.funkwhale.audio/funkwhale/funkwhale/-/blob/develop/api/funkwhale_api/radios/radios.py); [Funkwhale user docs: radios](https://docs.funkwhale.audio/user/radios/index.html)
- **Mastodon** [verified].
  - Home feed: joinmastodon.org says "No algorithms or ads to waste your time" and that you "receive their posts in chronological order". — [joinmastodon.org](https://joinmastodon.org/)
  - Trends: public endpoints `GET /api/v1/trends/tags` (3.0.0), `/trends/statuses` and `/trends/links` (3.5.0) return items ranked by "an internal trending score". — [Mastodon API: trends](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/methods/trends/)
  - The trend scores in the code are simple anomaly scores. For a status: observed = reblogs + favourites, expected = 1, score = (observed − expected)²/expected when observed ≥ 5, with a 1-hour half-life decay. For a tag: observed = distinct accounts using it today, expected = yesterday's count, with a 4-hour half-life on the max score. Items above a review threshold that are not yet approved generate staff review notifications. — [statuses.rb](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/blob/main/app/models/trends/statuses.rb); [tags.rb](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/blob/main/app/models/trends/tags.rb)
  - Follow suggestions: `GET /api/v2/suggestions` (3.4.0) returns "Accounts that are promoted by staff, or that the user has had past positive interactions with, but is not yet following". — [Mastodon API: suggestions](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/methods/suggestions/)
  - The earlier citation (docs.joinmastodon.org/admin/settings/#discovery) now returns 404 and has been replaced. The "relays share public posts" point remains [background, not re-verified].

### Inferences
- PeerTube has the greatest unmet need and the least prior work. Its built-in "same tag, newest first" list is a low bar. A browser-side or client-side recommender that exchanges profiles with consented peers would extend PeerTube Picks directly.
- Funkwhale's `similar` radio shows that an ActivityPub server can already do item-to-item CF from co-listening history while respecting per-user privacy levels. A peer-matched design would move that computation to the device and replace "everyone on my instance" with "peers I chose".
- ActivityPub has no private, consented data-sharing primitive beyond direct/followers-only posts. History exchange would need an out-of-band or encrypted channel.

### Gaps
- Resolved: PeerTube Picks is MIT-licensed, and its source is the solidheron repo (AMO listing).
- Usage numbers: the only figure found is about 4 average daily users for PeerTube Picks (AMO API, 2026-10-19). There are none for other PeerTube recommenders.

## Nostr: video clients, DVMs (NIP-90), Web-of-Trust scoring

### Takeaway
Nostr has the most developed "algorithm marketplace". NIP-90 Data Vending Machines (DVMs) let users request discovery feeds from competing providers, but the upstream NIPs repo now marks NIP-90 **unrecommended** ("this got totally out of control, prefer use-case-specific microstandards"). Trust services such as Vertex compute personalized PageRank over the follow graph. These systems rank by public follows and engagement, not by private watch history shared between peers. diVine (the Vine reboot, Nov 2025) is the most prominent Nostr video app. Its public issue tracker shows that recommendations come from a server-side Gorse recommender behind its "Funnelcake" REST API, and that swipe "feed tuning" signals are deliberately sent privately to that backend rather than published as Nostr events.

### Cited Findings
- **NIP-90 DVMs** [verified upstream]. A job marketplace: requests use kinds 5000–5999, results 6000–6999 (always request kind + 1000), and job feedback kind 7000. Providers compete on output and price. The upstream file is tagged `draft` `unrecommended` `optional`. — [NIP-90 (upstream)](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/90.md)
- Content discovery is a headline DVM use case. The NostrDVM framework lists discovery algorithms first, and its author expects the best algorithms to emerge from an open market. — [nostr.at note](https://nostr.at/note1npxzx3s94s8x5929qeyd4hkz58fplnyhye7sknh6s8lekq7dldgsmys8cw)
- Client support: Amethyst v0.87.2 added DVM discovery feeds. noStrudel and Coracle can use them via Vendata. — [No BS Bitcoin](https://nobsbitcoin.com/amethyst-v0-87-2); [Uncensored Tech "Bring Your Own Algo"](https://uncensoredtech.substack.com/p/bring-your-own-algo)
- What went wrong: discoverability of DVMs is poor. One operator saw zero jobs in 8 hours because most clients don't surface NIP-89 handler announcements. — [Stacker News](https://www.stacker.news/items/1463738)
- **NIP-90 status** [verified]. The upstream NIPs README lists it struck through: "~~NIP-90: Data Vending Machines~~ --- **unrecommended**: this got totally out of control, prefer use-case-specific microstandards". The 90.md file carries the same warning banner. This confirms the D-Central guide's claim. New work should not build on NIP-90 as a long-term standard. — [nips README](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/README.md); [D-Central](https://d-central.tech/nostr-data-vending-machines/)
- **Vertex** [verified]. "Web of Trust as a service" for Nostr. It continuously crawls follow lists and computes Monte Carlo Personalized PageRank. It is exposed as DVMs (Verify Reputation, Recommend Follows, Sort Authors), launched Feb 2025, and is MIT-licensed. — [No BS Bitcoin](https://www.nobsbitcoin.com/vertex-web-of-trust-as-a-service/)
- Related trust work: Relatr computes per-perspective trust. NIP-85 "trusted assertions" (kind 30382) let scoring services publish results to clients. OpenSats funds Web-of-Trust work. — [OpenSats WoT topic](https://opensats.org/topics/web-of-trust)
- **diVine** [verified]. A Vine reboot on Nostr, led by Evan "Rabble" Henshaw-Plath and funded by Jack Dorsey's nonprofit "and Other Stuff". It launched in Nov 2025 with a restored Vine archive (TechCrunch says more than 100,000 videos; other outlets say about 10,000, a conflict). It blocks suspected generative-AI uploads. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/12/jack-dorsey-funds-divine-a-vine-reboot-that-includes-vines-video-archive); [Cassius](https://cassiuslife.com/1268047/divine-everything-we-know-about-the-vine-reboot/); [werd.io](https://werd.io/jack-dorsey-funds-divine-a-vine-reboot-that-includes-vines-video-archive/)
  - Feed ranking [verified from diVine's own repos; formula not public]:
    - The divine-web README lists Home (following), Discovery, Trending/Popular, Hashtag, Categories and Profile feeds. "Funnelcake is the Divine REST API (`api.divine.video`)", used "alongside the relay for feeds and video metadata". Search uses NIP-50 relay-side sort modes `sort:hot`, `sort:top`, `sort:rising` and `sort:controversial`. — [divine-web README](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-web)
    - Epic #9415 (opened 22 Sep 2026, open) says the recommendation engine is **Gorse**, refitting hourly (`fit_period = "60m"`). The planned change makes left/right swipes demote or promote a creator or topic per request in Funnelcake. The swipe signal is "Recommendation input only" and must never feed moderation or reputation. — [divine-mobile #9415](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/issues/9415)
    - Issue #9419 replaces a public kind-4242 Nostr "feed tuning" event with a private NIP-98-authenticated POST to Funnelcake, because public swipe events would be "publicly attributable". The acceptance criterion is "Nothing is published to any relay". — [divine-mobile #9419](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/issues/9419)
    - Funnelcake's source is not public as far as I can tell. The epic links `divinevideo/divine-funnelcake`, but that repo is absent from the ecosyste.ms listing of the divinevideo org's 38 public repos. Gorse itself is open source (gorse-io/gorse, Go, Apache-2.0).
- Other Nostr video and media clients:
  - **Olas** [verified]. An Instagram-style Nostr client by pablof7z covering photos (kind:20 picture events) and short videos. — [No BS Bitcoin: Olas v0.1.4](https://www.nobsbitcoin.com/olas-v0-1-4/); [f7z.io](https://f7z.io/stuff/olas/)
  - zap.stream (live), Nostube and Tunestr (music) [background, not re-verified]. These are generally follow-based or DVM-fed feeds. — [zap.stream](https://zap.stream); [olas.app](https://olas.app)
- **NIP-51 lists** [verified; corrects the earlier claim]. Private list items are NIP-44-encrypted with a shared key "computed using the author's public and private key", meaning to the author's own key. They are private to the author, not shareable with chosen peers. NIP-51 can hold a user's own "liked/watched" list across devices. Sharing it with matched peers needs a different mechanism, such as NIP-44/NIP-59 gift-wrapped events addressed to each peer, or an MLS group via Marmot. — [NIP-51](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/51.md)

### Inferences
- Nostr is the most natural fit for this idea. Signed events, NIP-44 encryption and gift-wraps for private peer-to-peer data, pluggable ranking (DVMs, though NIP-90 is now "unrecommended" upstream), and WoT for spam resistance already exist.
- diVine's choice to keep swipe signals off public relays (#9419) is direct prior art for the privacy argument: even a large Nostr video app treats engagement signals as too sensitive to publish. The missing pieces are a convention for "watch/like history" events and a consented "match" relationship.
- Personalized PageRank over follows (Vertex) is a trust or popularity signal, not taste similarity. A "match" layer would add collaborative-filtering signal on top of it.

### Gaps
- I found no current usage figures for DVM feeds or diVine.
- Resolved: NIP-90 is marked unrecommended upstream.
- diVine's Trending/Popular formula and Funnelcake source are [unresolved] (backend appears private).

## AT Protocol / Bluesky: custom feeds, Graze, Skylight, Flashes, permissioned data

### Takeaway
ATproto has shipped the clearest working version of "algorithmic choice". Anyone can run a feed generator, and users subscribe to it. Graze turned this into a business. Video apps on ATproto (Skylight, Flashes) lean on human curators and custom feeds rather than a single For You algorithm. All ATproto repo data, including likes, is public. Permissioned data ("Atproto Spaces", proposal 0016) entered alpha in Aug 2026. It provides access control, not end-to-end encryption: the PDS and authorized apps can read the data. Graze has open-sourced a like-graph personalization engine (LinkLonk-style random walks), which is the closest public ATproto code to "people who like what you like".

## Middleware / "algorithmic choice" policy lineage (Fukuyama, Masnick, Free Our Feeds, Project Liberty/DSNP, Bluesky)

### Takeaway
There is a strong intellectual case, and some institutional backing, for separating recommendation from hosting ("middleware"). It has so far been realized mainly as ATproto custom feeds and Nostr DVMs. None of the proposals specifically envisions peer-to-peer taste sharing.

### Cited Findings
- Fukuyama argues that middleware, meaning third-party layers that curate or rank on top of platforms, is "the only solution to platform power" (2023). — [Carnegie Endowment](https://carnegieendowment.org/russia-eurasia/posts/2023/03/why-middleware-is-the-only-solution-to-platform-power)
- **Stanford Working Group on Platform Scale** [verified bibliographic details]. "Report of the Working Group on Platform Scale" by Francis Fukuyama, Barak Richman, Ashish Goel, Marietje Schaake, Roberta R. Katz and Douglas Melamed, 17 Nov 2020. The landing page does not itself mention middleware; the middleware recommendation is in the full PDF, which I did not re-read. — [Stanford Cyber Policy Center](https://cyber.fsi.stanford.edu/publication/report-working-group-platform-scale)
- **Masnick** [verified]. Mike Masnick, "Protocols, Not Platforms: A Technological Approach to Free Speech", Knight First Amendment Institute at Columbia University, *Free Speech Futures* essay series, 21 Aug 2019 (cited as 19-05 Knight First Amend. Inst.). — [Knight Institute](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/protocols-not-platforms-a-technological-approach-to-free-speech)
- **Project Liberty / DSNP** [verified]. Frank McCourt launched it in 2021 with $100M. DSNP is an open-source social-graph protocol "owned by the public". Project Liberty announced a partnership with **Free Our Feeds**, the effort to protect ATproto's independence. — [Project Liberty launch](https://www.projectliberty.io/news/civic-entrepreneur-frank-mccourt-launches-project-liberty-transformational-new-initiative-to-enable-healthier-more-equitable-online-economy/); [TechCrunch Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/10/open-web-initiatives-project-liberty-and-solid-could-be-teaming-up/)
- Bluesky's own "algorithmic choice" framing (2023). — [Bluesky blog](https://bsky.social/about/blog/3-30-2023-algorithmic-choice)
- **Free Our Feeds** [verified, self-description only]. "A campaign to support open, people-powered social media", an initiative of the Modal Foundation. Its three aims are open infrastructure, protection against capture through nonprofit governance, and real choice. The site gives no dated 2025–2026 status updates. — [freeourfeeds.com](https://freeourfeeds.com)

### Inferences
- A "match"-based recommender fits the middleware vision. It is user-chosen ranking whose inputs (peer histories) are controlled by the users themselves. It can be framed as the user-to-user form of middleware.

### Gaps
- I did not check the current activity of DSNP (Frequency chain) or Free Our Feeds' 2026 status.
